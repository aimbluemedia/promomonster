# 06 — Review Playbooks

The product's differentiator and its SEO engine. Generic software says "send a
request"; a playbook says who asks, when, in what words, and what to do when the
customer says yes but never does it.

## 1. The framework every playbook fills in

| Slot | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Trigger moment** | The point of peak satisfaction — usually earlier than owners think |
| **Who asks** | The person the customer just dealt with, not "the business" |
| **Primary channel** | What that customer will actually open |
| **The verbal ask** | What the technician or receptionist says out loud, first |
| **The message** | The text or email that follows, under 160 characters where possible |
| **Follow-up** | Exactly one, timed to the vertical |
| **Offline fallback** | QR card, invoice line, receipt — for customers who never respond digitally |
| **Objection** | The specific reason this vertical's customers hesitate |

Two rules hold across every vertical:

- **Ask everyone.** Not just the ones you expect to be happy. Required by Google's policies and by [05-compliance.md](05-compliance.md) §1, and it is also the only way the rating means anything.
- **The verbal ask comes first.** A text with no warning converts far worse than a text the customer was told to expect thirty seconds earlier. This is the single highest-leverage instruction in the entire product.

## 2. Landscaping / lawn care

- **Trigger** — the final walkthrough, standing on the finished work. Not that evening, not invoice day.
- **Who** — the crew lead who did the job.
- **Channel** — SMS. These customers are outdoors and rarely read email during the day.
- **Verbal ask** — *"If you're happy with how it turned out, I'll text you a link — it takes about thirty seconds and it really helps us."*
- **Message** — `Hi {first}, thanks for having us out today. If the yard looks good, would you mind leaving a quick review? {link} — {crew_lead}, {business}`
- **Follow-up** — once, 3 days later, morning.
- **Offline** — QR card clipped to the invoice or left in the door.
- **Objection** — "I don't know what to write." Answer it in the ask: *"even one line is fine."*

## 3. HVAC / plumbing / electrical

- **Trigger** — the moment the fix is demonstrated working, before packing up.
- **Who** — the technician.
- **Channel** — SMS.
- **Verbal ask** — *"You'll get a text from us in a bit — if I sorted you out today, a quick review means a lot. Mention my name if you like."*
- **Message** — `Hi {first}, glad we got that sorted today. A quick review helps other locals find us: {link} — {tech}, {business}`
- **Follow-up** — once, next day.
- **Offline** — QR on the invoice and on the van.
- **Objection** — emergency call-outs are stressful and the customer is thinking about the bill. Asking *before* the invoice conversation converts materially better.
- **Note** — naming the technician raises response and gives the owner per-tech data. It also makes the review more specific and therefore more persuasive.

## 4. Dental / medical / veterinary

- **Trigger** — at checkout, after treatment.
- **Who** — front desk. Never the clinician; it puts the patient in an awkward position.
- **Channel** — SMS or email, patient's choice.
- **Verbal ask** — *"If you've got a second later, a review really helps other people find us."*
- **Message** — `Hi {first}, thanks for coming in today. If you have a moment, a short review helps other patients find us: {link} — {practice}`
- **Follow-up** — once, 2 days later.
- **Offline** — QR on the appointment card.
- **Objection** — privacy. Patients hesitate to be publicly associated with a clinic. Say explicitly that they can keep it general and needn't mention treatment.
- **Compliance** — never mention a condition, procedure or anything treatment-specific in a message. **[HIPAA — counsel.]** This vertical needs its own review before launch.

## 5. Restaurants / cafés

- **Trigger** — with the bill, or on the receipt.
- **Who** — server or host.
- **Channel** — QR first. Phone numbers usually aren't collected, which changes the whole approach.
- **Verbal ask** — *"If you enjoyed it, there's a QR on the receipt — it takes a second."*
- **Message** — mostly not applicable; the QR *is* the campaign.
- **Follow-up** — none. There is no channel and chasing diners reads as desperate.
- **Offline** — QR on receipt, table card, and by the door at eye level on the way out.
- **Objection** — volume is high and each interaction is short, so conversion per customer is low but the customer count is enormous. Optimise placement, not persuasion.

## 6. Salons / barbers / spas

- **Trigger** — at the chair, while the client is looking in the mirror at the result.
- **Who** — the stylist who did the work.
- **Channel** — SMS.
- **Verbal ask** — *"If you love it, I'd really appreciate a quick review — I'll text you the link."*
- **Message** — `Hi {first}, hope you're still loving it! If you have a minute: {link} — {stylist} at {business}`
- **Follow-up** — once, 2 days later.
- **Offline** — QR at the till and on the mirror station.
- **Note** — this is the strongest per-stylist vertical in the set. Personal loyalty is high and the ask is natural.

## 7. Auto repair

- **Trigger** — at collection, when handing back the keys.
- **Who** — service advisor.
- **Channel** — SMS.
- **Verbal ask** — *"If we did right by you, a review helps a lot — I'll send a link."*
- **Message** — `Hi {first}, thanks for trusting us with the {vehicle}. A quick review helps other drivers find us: {link} — {business}`
- **Follow-up** — once, 3 days later.
- **Objection** — the customer just paid an unexpected bill. Ask at handover while relief is high, never with the invoice.

## 8. Law firms

- **Trigger** — at matter close, after the outcome is known.
- **Who** — the attorney, or the paralegal the client dealt with daily.
- **Channel** — email. More considered, and the right register.
- **Follow-up** — once, a week later.
- **Objection** — clients often will not want to be publicly associated with a legal matter. Say up front they can keep it entirely general.
- **Compliance** — **state bar advertising rules govern client testimonials and vary significantly.** Some states restrict what a firm may solicit or display. **[counsel]** — treat this vertical as gated until reviewed.

## 9. Real estate

- **Trigger** — at closing, or when handing over keys.
- **Who** — the agent.
- **Channel** — SMS, then email with photos.
- **Follow-up** — once, a week later.
- **Note** — transaction volume per agent is low but each review is worth more, and reviews attach to the agent as much as the brokerage. Personalisation matters more here than anywhere else.

will never do it consistently by hand is the most qualified lead you can get.

## 11. What turns these into the actual moat

Instrument every step. Record which trigger, channel, sender and timing produced
a completed review, per vertical, across every customer.

Within a year that answers questions no competitor can: whether naming the
technician really lifts HVAC response, whether the 3-day follow-up beats the
2-day, whether the crew lead outperforms the office. Then each playbook stops
being your best guess and becomes measured fact — and that improves every
customer's results at once.
