# 01 — Strategy

## 1. Positioning

> **PromoMonster — Get More Reviews. Get More Customers.**
> We show you exactly how to turn happy customers into Google reviews — then
> automate it.

Education first, software second, service third. The education is the
acquisition engine; the software is what makes the revenue recur.

**You are never selling reviews.** You are selling a repeatable process for
asking real customers, and the tooling that runs it. Say that plainly and often
— buyers in this market have been pitched by people who *were* selling reviews,
and the distinction is a selling point.

## 2. Why this is a better business than the click model

| | Click model | Reviews platform |
|---|---|---|
| Legitimacy | Violates platform terms; penalty lands on the customer | Legitimate; encouraged by Google |
| Payment processing | "Paid to click" is a shutdown category | Ordinary SaaS |
| Cold start | Needs a panel of thousands before selling anything | Sell on day one |
| Retention | Panel churns at $3/month earnings | Reviews compound; leaving loses the system |
| Value proven | Hard | Review count is visible on their own Google profile |
| Competitors | Grubby | Podium and Birdeye at $300–600/month |

The last row matters most. In the click model you were undercutting bot-traffic
sellers. Here you are undercutting funded companies charging $400 a month for
something you can deliver for $79.

## 3. What we sell, and to whom

### Three audiences

1. **Local service business owner** — "teach me how to get more reviews." The core buyer.
2. **Agency / freelancer** — "give me a system I can sell to my clients." Highest LTV, brings its own distribution.
3. **The owner's staff** — "teach my team how and when to ask." Not a buyer, but the reason the system works or fails, so training is part of the product.

Two deliberate changes from the draft:

- **$29 → $39 for the entry tier.** SMS registration costs make $29 thin to unprofitable once a customer sends anything (see [02-unit-economics.md](02-unit-economics.md) §4). Starter therefore excludes SMS; SMS begins at Growth, where it pays for itself.

## 4. The wedge: vertical playbooks

Generic review software tells a business to "send a request." That is not
advice, and it is why so many trials go cold.

PromoMonster asks what kind of business they are and produces a **specific
plan** — the moment to ask, the channel, the exact words, who on the team says
them, the offline fallback, and the follow-up timing. A landscaper and a dentist
get materially different plans because the transaction shape is different.

This is the differentiator, and it happens to be defensible: it is judgement and
accumulated data, not a feature a competitor ships in a sprint. It is also the
best SEO asset in the business — every vertical is a page people search for.

See [06-playbooks.md](06-playbooks.md) for the content itself.

## 5. Review Growth Score

A single 0–100 number on the dashboard, built from what is actually configured
and happening:

```
Google review link configured        ✅
QR code created and printed          ✅
Request sending enabled              ✅
Follow-up enabled                    ❌
Team trained (checklist complete)    ❌
Reviews answered within 48 hours     ✅
Website widget installed             ❌
Asking every customer, not some      ✅

Your next best action → Turn on the 3-day follow-up
```

It gives a reason to log in when nothing needs doing, it makes the value visible
before the reviews arrive, and "Next Best Action" is the natural in-product
upsell surface. Keep it honest: a score that only ever goes up is decoration.

## 6. Competition

| | Price | Where we win |
|---|---|---|
| **Podium** | ~$400–600/mo | Price, and they're sold to larger multi-location businesses |
| **Birdeye** | ~$300–500/mo | Price; ours is self-serve without a sales call |
| **NiceJob** | ~$75–200/mo | Vertical playbooks and the education funnel |
| **GatherUp / Grade.us** | ~$99–200/mo | Agency-focused; we serve the owner directly too |
| **Jobber / Housecall Pro / ServiceTitan** | Bundled | **The real threat** — see below |
| **Doing nothing** | $0 | The actual default, and the biggest competitor |

*Verify all competitor pricing before using it in external material.*

**The threat worth taking seriously is bundling.** Field-service platforms
already include review requests for home-services businesses. If a roofer runs
Jobber, review requests are a checkbox they already own.

Three responses, all of which the plan supports:

1. **Go where bundling isn't.** Dentists, salons, law firms, restaurants, auto repair and real estate are far less covered than home services.
2. **Compete on the part bundles don't do** — the playbook, the training, the score, the reply drafting. Bundled tools send a text; they don't teach a business how to ask.
3. **Integrate rather than fight.** Importing completed jobs from those systems makes you a companion, not a replacement.

## 7. Go to market

**Content is the engine.** The vertical playbooks are simultaneously the product
and the SEO strategy: `/how-landscapers-get-google-reviews`,
`/review-request-text-templates-for-roofers`,
`/when-should-a-dentist-ask-for-a-review`. High intent, low competition,
and each page ends in the free audit.

**The free Review Audit is the conversion mechanism.** Enter a business name,
get a real report: current rating and count, how that compares to the three
nearest competitors, review velocity over the last 90 days, how many go
unanswered, and the biggest gap. It is genuinely useful, it is delivered
instantly, and it produces exactly the anxiety the product resolves. Nothing
else in the funnel converts like showing someone their competitor has four times
their reviews.

**Then, in order:** your existing SearchMonster and MonsterList customer lists
(warmest audience, zero cost) · the partner programme · targeted cold outreach
opening with the audit rather than a pitch · niche communities where these
owners already are.

## 8. Roadmap

**Phase 0 — weeks 1–8. Sell before building.**
Landing pages, the free audit run **by hand**, three vertical playbooks as PDFs,
and the toolkit sold via a payment link. Target: 20 paying customers and 5
completed done-for-you setups. What they ask for is the specification.

**Phase 1 — months 2–6. The core loop.**
Account signup, Google Place connection, review link and QR generation, contact
import, email requests, request tracking, review monitoring, Growth Score, and
the automated audit. SMS lands here only once 10DLC onboarding is built.

**Phase 2 — months 6–10.**
SMS with full consent handling, follow-up sequences, website widget, reply
drafting, multi-location, team accounts, partner programme.

**Phase 3 — year two.**
Integrations with field-service platforms, certification, reporting API,
additional review platforms.

## 9. Long-term

The durable asset is **the playbook data**: which ask, on which channel, at
which moment, converts best for each vertical — measured across thousands of
real campaigns. Software is copyable; knowing that a roofer's request converts
at 34% when the foreman sends it from the driveway and 11% when the office sends
it the next day is not.

That data improves every customer's results, feeds the content engine, and
eventually supports benchmarking as a product of its own.
